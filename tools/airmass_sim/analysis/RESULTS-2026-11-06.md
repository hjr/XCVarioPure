## Findings on the way

- **Gyro gating** in the logs is the device default 0.5 °/s (24–46 % of the gyro values zero); in the simulator it costs
  AirMass ≤ 0.01 m/s. Only ~2 °/s would make it lose to TE.
- **The pitot GNSS reference favours TE netto**: in the simulator TE correlates better with it than AirMass (0.971 vs
  0.964), against the truth the reverse (0.959 vs 0.973).
- **The kinetic blend** (inertial acceleration instead of the airspeed change in gusts) helps in straight flight on
  real flights but hurt in circling (10 s: 0.79 with, 0.63 without); in the simulator it helps in circling, even in
  rough air. Hence the bank fade.
- **Not the cause** of the circling problem: IMU mounting misalignment (≤ 2° roll/yaw, simulator), the AoA scale limit
  (stuck at 0.8 on 9 of 11 flights; relaxing it changed nothing), the logged accelerometer low pass (undoing it: ≤ 0.02),
  the wind work term (removing it is worse), a heading-tied attitude pitch error of the observed ±0.5° (simulator:
  would need ~6°).
- **Around the circle**: the IMU acceleration vs airspeed change disagreement has a first harmonic of 0.06–0.09 m/s²;
  AirMass's pitch vs the device AHRS differs by ±0.5°, tied to absolute heading (peak ~40–70°), same for left and right
  circles. Source unknown (neither attitude uses the compass; wind direction confounded, 230–300° on 9 of 11 flights).
- **Real airspeed** changes ~3× more over 1 s than in the simulator (0.80–0.88 vs 0.29 m/s rms, straight flight).
- **Mass**: 430 kg vs the logged ballast (~440 kg) changes nothing (0.632 either way). Since later the same day every
  script replays at the logged weight (441 kg, 450 kg on two flights): the numbers move by 0.002 m/s at most
  (`raw/summary_real_logged_mass.txt`, `raw/needle_real_logged_mass.txt`), and the AoA scale still ends at its 0.8 limit
  on 8 of 11 flights (0.2 to 1.0 without the limit), so the weight is not why.
- **Conditions** explain < 3 % of the error variance; AirMass reads more than TE in left turns (+0.05, +0.20 above 40°
  bank) than in right turns (+0.02).

## Needle smoothness and speed (later the same day)

With the blend faded out from 20° bank, the airspeed derivative reached the needle while circling. Goal: a needle as
steady as TE netto's and at least 1 s faster. New defaults:
- the blend fades from full at 10° to none at 50° bank (`blend_bank = 30`, `blend_bank_width = 40`: 25 % at 40°);
- as it fades out, a complementary filter takes over (`circling_blend_tau = 0.5 s`): the inertial acceleration for what
  changes faster than 0.5 s, the airspeed derivative for the rest. The full blend let the inertial acceleration in below
  `kinetic_tau` (8 s), so the attitude error around the circle (period ~20 s) passed almost entirely;
- the vario output gets a low pass of up to 0.5 s, faded in the same way (`circling_tau`).

## How good is the reference? (later the same day)

The gust-insensitive reference takes AirMass's averaged wind, and a wind error δ makes it swing once per circle by
about 0.8·δ m/s: shifting the wind by 1 m/s moved it by 0.40 m/s rms over 10 s while circling (0.06 straight), and
raised both AirMass's and TE netto's circling errors by 0.07–0.15. A wind from GNSS alone (`circle_ref.js`: per full
turn, the ground velocities fitted to a circle around the wind) instead: TE netto, which uses no wind, agrees less with
that reference (10 s circling 0.894, 0.679 with the fit taking the measured airspeed, against 0.642), so AirMass's wind
is the better one and the page keeps it. The GNSS-only fit confirms AirMass's wind (1.20 m/s rms apart per turn, mean
difference under 0.1 m/s) and its airspeed calibration (turn radius over the calibrated TAS: median 0.998).

Each series' own error, without the reference's, by the three-cornered hat (AirMass, TE netto and the reference as
three measurements with independent errors; `raw/circle_ref.txt`), rms m/s, cockpit AirMass (straight / circling):

| | 10 s | 30 s |
|---|---|---|
| AirMass | **0.19** / 0.28 | 0.18 / 0.14 |
| TE netto | 0.42 / **0.24** | 0.17 / **0.11** |
| the reference itself | 0.43 / 0.60 | 0.23 / 0.17 |

The same with the GNSS-only reference (AirMass 0.19 / 0.30, TE netto 0.42 / 0.21 over 10 s), so the split is not the
reference's doing. Over 10 s the reference is the largest error: the 0.47 / 0.66 m/s of the tables above are mostly
it. AirMass and TE netto share the static pressure and nearly the polar, which the hat would count to the reference, so
their own errors are, if anything, a little larger.

## The circling attitude error (later the same day)

Ideas from a Codex review (read-only, `raw/codex_circling_ideas.md`) and tests:

- **Part of the circling gap was the scoring**: every series was shifted by its delay over the whole flight, but
  AirMass is 0.8 s late overall and 1.4 s while circling (TE netto 2.4 / 2.6 s); the lift changes around each circle,
  so the misfit delay counts as error. Shifted by the delay of each phase (`needle.js PHASE_LAG=1`), own errors over
  10 s circling: AirMass 0.259 (was 0.280), TE netto 0.228 (0.234); 30 s 0.130 / 0.107. The gap is 0.03 m/s.
- **Not a wind gradient** (climbing through a changing wind, which the attitude's gravity reference ignores: a ground
  fixed tilt of dW/dt / g): over 118 thermals the wind changes 0.015 m/s² rms (too small for the 0.06–0.09 m/s²
  disagreement), and AirMass's once-per-circle error does not follow it (correlation 0.06, `shear.js`). Nor the wind
  (−0.16, as TE netto −0.18). Its direction varies from thermal to thermal (mean 0.09 m/s against 0.37 rms).
- `wind_work_hp`: none 0.400, 30 s 0.332, 10 s (kept) 0.280 own circling error.
- `circling_blend_tau` 0.45: 0.257 vs 0.259 with the per-phase delays, a needle less steady than TE netto: not taken.
- The attitude's flight path acceleration lacked V·α′·du/dα (a changing angle of attack turns the air velocity in the
  body): added, real flights unchanged (0.261 vs 0.259 circling, 0.201 vs 0.199 straight, circling delay 1.6 s vs 1.4),
  simulator slightly better (entry rms 0.493 → 0.487, held-out 0.473 → 0.465): reverted, no gain where it matters.
- Not tried: a heading-locked harmonic fit of the acceleration disagreement (the error's direction is not stable
  across thermals, and it could take out real air structure), a signed sideslip (needs a Recording flight).
