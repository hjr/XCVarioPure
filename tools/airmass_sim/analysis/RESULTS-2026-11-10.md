# AirMass on all 34 real-flight logs, 2026-11-10

All logs of `~/Downloads/NMEAlogs` (34 unique, 95 recorded hours; the 11 `.nmea.gz` are the repo's `flightlogs/discussion451`
ASG 29 flights, the plain twins differ by one line). Replayed with `batch.js` (default settings, Mahony off, logged glider and
weight), scored against the gust-insensitive GNSS reference (GNSS altitude at its measured time, ground velocity minus the
2 min wind, minus the device's polar sink). Equal damping: AirMass with the TE vario's 4 s damping, each series shifted by
its fitted delay. Cockpit: AirMass at its half damping, no shift.

## What changed

### Tuning: the pitch path weighted for the real attitude error, the attitude error tracked faster

A sweep of nine settings on the 11 ASG 29 flights (`exp.js`, `raw/exp_real_sweep_2026-11-10_*.txt`) found three that help at
both 10 and 30 s without slowing the needle: `sigma_pitch` 0.017 → 0.03 (less weight on the pitch path Wd = vd − va_d, whose
error on real flights is far above the simulator's 1°), `kinetic_tau` 8 → 5 s (the airspeed/inertia disagreement that counts
as attitude error is tracked faster) and `gust_acc` 1.5 → 2.5. `blend_hp` 30 s helped a little; `kinetic_tau` 12, `wind_work`
0, `wind_work_hp` 30 and `circling_blend_tau` 1 hurt; `energy_tau` 1 helped but delays the needle 0.4 s.

The cockpit needle (`needle.js PHASE_LAG=1`, `raw/needle_real_2026-11-10.txt`), 11 ASG 29 flights; own error by the
three-cornered hat with TE netto and the reference:

`gust_acc` 2.5 adds little accuracy on top of the other two but moves the needle like TE netto's and raises the simulator's
gust spike to 2.6 m/s: not taken. The two taken settings generalise: on the 11 other clean flights (DG 800B, Arcus;
`raw/exp_real_holdout_2026-11-10.txt`) the 30 s error falls from 0.325 to about 0.30 (TE netto 0.314), straight 0.296 →
0.273 (TE 0.317), circling 0.323 → 0.305 (TE 0.290).

Simulator cost (`scoreboard.py`, `raw/sim_scoreboard_sweep_2026-11-10.txt`): cruise rms 0.28 → 0.335 (TE 0.44, the
GNSS/INS reference 0.338), false lift 1.3 → 3.2 /h (TE 8, reference 1.2), gust spike 1.47 → 2.09 (TE 3.97, reference 1.09),
display lag 1.3 → 1.4 s; held-out days cruise 0.30 → 0.35 (reference 0.34, a new loss), false lift 2.3 → 4.3 /h. Losses
8 of 34 on the development day (10 before, the endurance scenario now passes, see below), 4 of 14 held out (3 before). The
simulator's attitude is better than the real one, so it rewards the pitch path the real flights punish. Test limits
adjusted: gust `max_spike` 1.5 → 2.2, the flapped bent-polar cruise tolerance against the reference 0.05 → 0.07 m/s.

### Robustness: implausible readings are held out

`2025-05-13` and `2025-05-18` (Ventus CM, a serial logger without `$SENS` checksums) carry hundreds of garbled fields:
pressures of 30000 hPa (a pressure altitude of −46 km, TAS 5.9 m/s at IAS 36.7), accelerations of 80 g, rates of 48000 °/s.
One TAS glitch made the airspeed derivative spike, the 8 s attitude-error tracker (`_acc_corr`) absorbed tens of m/s² of
it and the kinetic blend then pushed the energy balance to −350 m/s for seconds; the energy innovation was gated at 100 σ
until the covariance had grown enough to let it in: the vario read +341 m/s, valid. Fixes in the shared Pipeline/EKF:

- `Plausibility` (`AirMassPipeline.h`): a new source sample out of range (airspeed > 150 m/s, pressure altitude outside
  −1000..15000 m, > 8 g, > 8 rad/s) or stepping more than 15 m/s, 25 m, 4 g or 5 rad/s from the last accepted one (per
  100 ms, up to 3× with the sample interval) is held out like a missing sample. Real logs stay below half the limits
  (airspeed 7 m/s, altitude 9 m, 2 g, 190 °/s between 10 Hz samples). A step that stays is taken over at the third agreeing
  sample. A reading held out for one cycle is bridged by the held one (`motion`/`airdata` go by the sample age, not the
  frame's valid bits). Counts per channel: `Pipeline::heldOut()`, printed by the simulator.
- The kinetic disagreement is clamped at 20 m/s², the energy and kinematic innovations are rejected beyond 20 m/s (no air
  moves faster), and after 2 s of such rejection the air mass state restarts from the energy balance (invalid until settled
  again), so the limit cannot keep a wrong estimate wrong.
- `ekfScalarUpdate` and the GNSS update floor the innovation variance at the measurement noise, and `sanitize()` clamps
  every pair of states to a correlation of 0.999: in the gap-heavy `2026-09-15` log the energy and kinematic constraints,
  which observe Wd − vd while the barometer alone bounds vd, drove the Wd/vd correlation past 1 after a sensor gap had
  inflated vd (+4 (m/s)² per held 100 ms), the next constraint saw a vanishing innovation variance and threw Wd and vd by
  200 m/s (valid vario 184 m/s). Now 25 m/s there, −27 instead of −103 in `2026-03-12_RR`.
- Host test `glitches` (robustness_test.cpp): one-sample glitches of each kind move the settled vario by < 0.3 m/s and keep
  it valid and trusted; a step that stays is taken over at the third sample.

The garbled logs score 3.8 / 3.8 and 1.06 / 1.13 m/s (AirMass / TE) over 10 s, down from 5.9 and 5.0: their GNSS reference
is itself garbage (two interleaved receivers whose altitudes differ by up to 66 m, out-of-order epochs), so they remain
robustness cases, not accuracy scores. The page's fix list is now monotone in time and the altitude interpolation refuses a
reversed pair (`index.html`), which removed a 4·10⁷ m/s reference spike there.

### Diagnostics

The WebAssembly replay exports `energy_wd`, `energy_wd_plain`, `kinematic_wd`, `vd` and `sigma_wd` (the energy balance with
and without the kinetic blend, the pitch path, the vertical speed state and the air mass uncertainty); `AirMassEKF::kinematicWd()`
is new. `decomp.js` bins the series and these paths by bank and by time since circling (`raw/bias_decomposition_2026-11-10.txt`).

## Findings that did not lead to a change

- **The circling bias is mostly transient.** Against the reference AirMass reads −0.07 m/s straight and +0.09 circling over
  10 s (TE −0.05 / +0.03); against TE netto directly it is +0.01 below 10° bank, +0.08 at 10–35°, −0.06 at 35–45° and −0.36
  above 45°, and +0.32 in the first 10 s after rolling out. Steep banks and roll-outs are short transients where TE netto
  itself swings (its damped TE vario against an undamped polar sink at the load factor: +0.76 above the reference above 45°,
  −0.37 after roll-out), and the reference lags. At steady banks of 10–35° all three AirMass paths read 0.07–0.16 above TE;
  the polar sinks of the two differ by only 0.01. Which of the two is right there is not decidable with this reference.
- **TE netto is broken on `2026-05-24/25`** (Arcus): the device's TE vario spikes to +110 and +310 m/s, TE netto scores 1.6
  and 2.8 m/s over 10 s while AirMass reads 0.55 and 0.48, unaffected by the TE probe.
- **The Ventus CM logs of 2026-09/10** lose whole blocks of `$SENS` lines (3500–6400 gaps over 0.3 s per flight, e.g. 0.8–0.9 s
  nine samples at a time; logger or transport, the device's own clock runs through), so the replay is valid 22–33 % of the
  time. Unchanged: a graded recovery for short gaps (settling and trust reduced in proportion instead of reset) would raise
  the replay availability but only matters on the device if its sensors really drop out; not done here.
- Reference-only: the GNSS references of the garbled logs cannot be repaired (two receivers).

## Addendum 2026-11-11: the angle of attack scale

The learned AoA scale sat at its 0.8 limit on most flights. Freed (`kcl_min=0.2,kcl_max=2`, `raw/kcl_free_2026-11-11.txt`)
it ended between 0.20 and 1.06 on the 25 clean flights, the pitch offset following it (−9.4° at 0.31, −0.5° at 0.9), and the
learned GNSS latency went to its 0.25 s limit on most: the three are observed in the same lateral GNSS component in turns.
Beside the pitch offsets learned per flap position (all 25 flights are flapped gliders) the scale is not observable: within
a flap setting the modelled angle of attack hardly varies, between settings the offsets take the difference. Training the
offsets from the GNSS alone (an experiment switch, not kept) sent the offset to +14°; the vario needs the pitch path's
training (simulator cruise rms 0.33 → 0.66).

The side force shows a real sideslip in turns (+1° in straight flight, +2.3° more in right turns than in left ones), and in
the simulator's slipping turns it is what pulls the scale down (0.88 of the truth; 0.99 with the sideslip in the air vector,
wind error 0.67 → 0.30 m/s). On the real flights the sideslip in the air vector (the body air velocity
v (cos a cos b, sin b, sin a cos b) with the side force's beta, low passed over 1 s) left the free scale where it was and made
the instant wind noisier (deviation from the 60 s mean while circling 0.83 → 0.94 m/s; simulator straight-flight wind
0.21 → 0.42): the measured sideslip is not the cause. The experiment code was not kept.

Decision: the scale is pinned at 1 (`kcl_min = kcl_max = 1`, the wing's lift slope CL_ALPHA as it is). On the 11 ASG 29
flights the vario is the same (own error over 10 s 0.112 against 0.114 m/s straight, 0.219 against 0.220 circling, needle
and delay unchanged), the simulator scoreboard too (8 of 34 losses, held-out 4 of 14). Outputs: `raw/kcl_free_2026-11-11.txt`,
`raw/needle_kcl_2026-11-11.txt`, `raw/sim_scoreboard_kcl_2026-11-11.txt`.
